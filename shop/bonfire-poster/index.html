The work behind the print

Follow the piece from Rocco’s first pencil sketches through thousands of white marks on black, then into the BeeInk workshop where every sheet was pulled and checked by hand.

  1. Searching for the scene

    The first pages explore the gathering in loose graphite, testing how its animals, fires and landscape might hold together.

  2. Making one shared landscape

    Rocco then drew the entire gathering in graphite. The fires, trees and open sky give many separate figures a common rhythm.

  3. From graphite to black

    With the composition beside him, Rocco moved to the final black surface. The pencil drawing remained the map; the finished texture would come from white linework.

1 / 3 · SWIPE OR USE THE ARROWS

  1. One animal at a time

    Short strokes build weight and direction. The bear comes forward first while the rest of the scene remains only a set of quiet outlines.

  2. Light before detail

    Dense linework turns the upper edge into a night sky. Below it, the untouched stag shows how much planning remains visible before each area is filled.

  3. Every mark has a direction

    At this scale, the method becomes clear: repeated lines curve around bodies, describe fur and separate one animal from the next.

  4. A gathering within the gathering

    A second fire appears higher in the landscape. Even this small corner becomes its own scene, surrounded by trees and watched over by the larger figures below.

  5. The community fills in

    The central fire finally joins the bear, stag and smaller neighbours. Separate pockets of activity now read as one connected landscape.

  6. Almost every part is speaking

    The final pass balances the bright areas, deep shadows and small transitions that let the eye move through such a dense scene.

  7. The forest, complete

    At a distance the drawing becomes one bold black-and-white image. Up close, it opens back into the thousands of individual marks it’s made of.

1 / 7 · SWIPE OR USE THE ARROWS

  1. From paper to ink

    At BeeInk, the finished artwork moves from the drawing board into a screen-printing setup. The image-bearing screen is aligned over the paper so every sheet meets it in the same place.

  2. Ink across the mesh

    Black ink is worked across the prepared screen with a squeegee. Even pressure carries the image through the open areas of the mesh and onto the paper below.

  3. The forest appears on paper

    As the screen lifts, the dense woodland returns as a single black impression, surrounded by the wide white margin of the final print.

  4. A careful hand-off

    The fresh sheet is lifted by its edges, keeping hands clear of the wet ink and preserving the fine marks carried over from Rocco’s original.

  5. Every pull is checked

    Ink coverage, edges and placement are inspected before the sheet leaves the press. The dark field must stay rich without losing the smallest white details.

  6. Sheet after sheet

    The completed prints are laid out separately while the ink settles and dries. The singular drawing has become a physical run of hand-pulled impressions.

  7. Made to live on paper

    The finished 35 × 50 cm print preserves the fine linework on heavy 300gsm stock. Every copy in the edition of 250 is signed and numbered by Rocco.

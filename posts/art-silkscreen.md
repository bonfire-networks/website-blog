## A collaboration from the beginning

Bringing these prints into the world was a shared effort, sustained by the cooperation between Bonfire, artist and illustrator [Rocco Lombardi](https://roccolombardi.bigcartel.com), and the artisans at [BeeInk](https://beeinkserigrafia.it), a screen-printing and graphics workshop rooted in the Casa dei Beni Comuni in Belluno.

Rocco’s collaboration with Bonfire goes back to the project’s very beginnings. He created the Bonfire logo and many of the illustrations that have accompanied us throughout the years. His vision and style have never been merely decorative: they have remained a source of inspiration and an important reference point for Bonfire’s identity and roots.

For this edition, that long-standing visual collaboration entered a new phase. Rocco’s artwork was brought into the hands of BeeInk, whose craft and experience transformed it into a physical, hand-printed edition. Bonfire and its community provided the support that allowed the process to unfold.

Each of us contributed different skills, time, and care and the work could not have existed without the cooperation among all three. Our heartfelt thanks go to Rocco and BeeInk for their commitment, generosity, and patience throughout this collaboration.

These prints are more than rewards. They are a tangible part of the collective effort that continues to sustain Bonfire, an effort rooted in the belief that communities should be able to shape and govern their own digital spaces.

### From the screen to the silkscreen

Silkscreen printing, also known as screen printing or serigraphy, is a hands-on printing technique in which ink is pressed through a fine mesh screen prepared with a stencil of the image. When an artwork contains multiple colours, each one is printed separately, one layer at a time, using its own screen.

![A printer spreading ink across the screen by hand](/img/shop/beeink-printing/04-inking-the-screen.jpg)
*BeeInk work in process made during the creation of the bonfire screenprint*

The process gives every print a rich, tactile quality. Small variations can emerge as the ink is applied, meaning that each copy carries subtle traces of how it was made. Rather than simply reproducing an image, silkscreen printing turns it into a physical object shaped by materials, gestures, and time.

![A printer finishing a print by hand, the artwork emerging from the ink](/img/shop/beeink-printing/01-screen-setup.jpg)
*Rocco work starts emerging from the ink*

For this edition, Rocco’s artwork travelled from its original form into the hands of BeeInk, where it was prepared and printed by hand as a limited silkscreen edition. It felt like a fitting process for Bonfire: collaborative, material, and built layer by layer.

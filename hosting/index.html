Cooperative Hosting Network.

Your community keeps control of its Bonfire space. Cooperative partners handle the servers, setup, and technical support.

Find a hosting partner Help build the network

Why cooperative hosting

Own your community's space. Share the infrastructure.

You shouldn't have to become a sysadmin to own your community's space—or hand it to a Big Tech landlord. Self-hosting can offer autonomy, but going it alone often means relying on the same giant cloud providers, with every small instance duplicating infrastructure and energy use.

What matters is who governs the infrastructure, not simply where the machines sit. Cooperative hosting offers a third path: shared, community-owned infrastructure operated for mutual benefit.

The Cooperative Hosting Network is an open invitation to build that path together: a sustainable, resilient ecosystem for Bonfire hosting, development, and technical support.

The model

We're looking for more participants to join in building a sustainable and resilient cooperative hosting ecosystem in the face of digital landscapes dominated by profit-driven giants. Our model, inspired by Open Cooperative Ecosystems and Community Supported Agriculture (CSA), treats the software, the infrastructure it runs on, and the communities that use it as parts of one system that sustains itself. We see it as a way to keep not-for-profit free software projects alive, while avoiding the traps of SaaS (software as a service) or other models.

Such a cooperative alliance brings together many kinds of people, all pitching in: users, communities, moderators, tech supporters, infrastructure operators, translators, designers, developers, and more. Participation and contribution can come in various forms: financial support, resource sharing, labour, and active participation in decision-making and project upkeep.

This approach based on solidarity and mutual aid would ensure resiliency, accessibility and diversity, while promoting open community involvement. It puts trust, transparency, and people's control over their own tools back at the heart of how the software gets made.

The Cooperative Hosting Network would greatly simplify the setup, hosting, and operation of a Bonfire instance through an automated install wizard and dashboard, which would act as a gateway to an ecosystem of developers, designers, hosting providers, and other relevant services, which would work together to provide users and communities with:

OpennessDesigners, developers, and sysadmins, among other workers, can join the ecosystem to provide services and receive compensation.
MutualismParticipants that meet specific criteria may exchange services in-kind, and benefit from special rates or pro-bono services.
FlexibilityFrom a personal instance to a large community, the hosting ecosystem will guide the user based on their specific needs and budget.
InclusivityUsers and communities can collectively shape the ecosystem's roadmaps, by co-designing and funding desired features.

The dashboard would also double as a decentralized economic network, transparently recording every contribution and distributing funds based on value equations democratically defined by the ecosystem stakeholders.
